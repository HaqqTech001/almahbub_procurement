# HAMD Product DNA

**HAMD Genesis**  
**Public brand:** Almahbub International  
**Technology partner:** HAQQ TECH

## Purpose

HAMD Product DNA defines the recognizable personality that must persist across
every product surface, feature, message, workflow, and interaction. It is a
decision filter for building a coherent global procurement platform-not a
marketing slogan.

When a proposed design, feature, piece of content, or automation conflicts
with this document, the DNA takes precedence.

## The HAMD promise

**HAMD makes international procurement feel calm, clear, and controlled.**

The platform does not merely show information. It reduces uncertainty by
showing what is true now, who owns the next action, what the user can do, and
what will happen next.

## The eight traits

### 1. Calm

**Meaning:** HAMD removes visual and operational noise so users can make
important business decisions without stress.

**Behavior**
- One clear primary action per context.
- Quiet surfaces, deliberate hierarchy, and restrained color.
- Critical exceptions are prominent without becoming alarming.
- Complex details are available, but not forced into every view.

**Never**
- Fill dashboards with equally weighted widgets.
- Use decorative motion, aggressive alerts, countdown pressure, or visual
  clutter.
- Make a user hunt for the current status or next action.

**Test:** Can a first-time user identify the page purpose and next action in
five seconds?

### 2. Trustworthy

**Meaning:** Every page should make users feel that HAMD is accountable,
competent, and honest.

**Behavior**
- Show commercial assumptions, timestamps, ownership, source records, and
  meaningful status definitions.
- Separate confirmed facts from estimates and recommendations.
- Preserve decision history for quotes, approvals, payments, and shipment
  events.
- Use precise language instead of vague reassurance.

**Never**
- Promise a delivery date, cost, payment protection, or customs result that is
  not contractually and operationally certain.
- Hide exclusions, fees, quote expiry, or uncertainty in secondary content.
- Use ambiguous statuses such as “processing” without context.

**Test:** Could an informed user explain why a record has its current state and
what evidence supports it?

### 3. Intelligent

**Meaning:** HAMD anticipates useful work and reduces effort while retaining
human responsibility.

**Behavior**
- Suggest next actions, identify incomplete requests, summarize long
  conversations, and surface potential risks.
- Use AI to draft, explain, classify, retrieve, and organize-not to make
  unreviewed commercial, financial, or operational commitments.
- Cite the source record behind meaningful AI answers and recommendations.

**Never**
- Simulate certainty, invent facts, hide AI involvement, or create automation
  that bypasses approvals.
- Replace a knowledgeable procurement officer with generic chatbot behavior.

**Test:** Does this intelligence make a real task faster, safer, or easier to
understand without removing human control?

### 4. Human

**Meaning:** HAMD communicates like an experienced, respectful operations team
rather than a faceless system.

**Behavior**
- Use direct, warm, plain business language.
- Write messages with a fact, impact, next action, owner, and update time.
- Attribute communication clearly to people, teams, system events, or AI.
- Let users ask for help in the context of the work they are doing.

**Never**
- Use robotic confirmation text, blame users for errors, or hide a problem
  behind generic “something went wrong” language.
- Pretend automated messages are human-written.

**Test:** Would a buyer feel informed and respected after receiving this
message during a delay or payment issue?

### 5. Global

**Meaning:** HAMD feels ready for international trade without making every
interaction feel complicated.

**Behavior**
- Support multi-currency, country-specific addresses, time zones, languages,
  units, documents, and long localized strings.
- Use internationally understandable icons, date/time context, and
  procurement/trade terminology with explanation where needed.
- Make country, route, currency, supplier, and delivery context visible when
  it affects a decision.

**Never**
- Assume one currency, date format, tax model, shipping route, language, or
  local business convention.
- Present a global experience as a decorative map without operational depth.

**Test:** Can this workflow work for a buyer and supplier in different
countries without hidden assumptions?

### 6. Efficient

**Meaning:** HAMD respects the user’s time and removes unnecessary repeated
work.

**Behavior**
- Keep frequent actions close to the relevant record.
- Preserve entered information, support drafts/templates/reorders, and make
  filters/search actionable.
- Use progressive disclosure instead of long linear forms.
- Design for keyboard, shortcuts, bulk actions, and contextual actions in
  professional workflows.

**Never**
- Make users copy data between screens, revisit completed steps, or navigate
  away from the context to perform an obvious action.
- Hide essential operations behind obscure menus.

**Test:** Is there a safe way to remove at least one click, one repeated field,
or one context switch?

### 7. Transparent

**Meaning:** Users always understand what is happening to their request,
money, shipment, documents, and data.

**Behavior**
- Distinguish draft, submitted, awaiting confirmation, confirmed, estimated,
  delayed, completed, and failed states.
- Show process milestones, timestamps, responsible party, and next expected
  event.
- Explain irreversible actions before confirmation and provide an audit trail
  afterward.
- Make permissions and unavailable actions understandable.

**Never**
- Collapse a multi-step process into a single broad progress label.
- Treat an initiated payment as confirmed or an estimated ETA as a guaranteed
  delivery date.

**Test:** Can the user tell what has happened, what has not happened, and what
will happen next?

### 8. Purposeful premium

**Meaning:** HAMD feels premium because every visual and interaction detail is
intentional, not because it is visually expensive.

**Behavior**
- Use excellent typography, spacing, data legibility, empty/loading/error
  states, and accessible feedback.
- Make high-value decisions feel focused and composed.
- Favor durable, simple patterns over trend-driven interface decoration.

**Never**
- Use glass effects, gradients, animation, iconography, or illustrations where
  they weaken clarity or performance.
- Resemble a generic admin template, a legacy ERP, or a consumer ecommerce
  promotion.

**Test:** Does the design improve comprehension, confidence, or speed? If not,
remove it.

## The three-question rule

Every screen, modal, notification, and workflow must answer:

1. **Where am I?**  
   Provide clear page identity, record context, and relevant status.
2. **What can I do?**  
   Expose allowed primary and secondary actions in a predictable location.
3. **What should I do next?**  
   Prioritize the highest-value safe next action, its owner, and any deadline.

If any answer is unclear, the screen is incomplete.

## Product voice

HAMD voice is concise, factual, respectful, and composed.

- Prefer: “Your quote is ready for review. It expires 14 June at 17:00 WAT.”
- Avoid: “Great news! Your amazing quote is waiting for you!”
- Prefer: “Customs clearance is delayed. Our logistics team is verifying the
  missing document and will update you by 14:00 WAT.”
- Avoid: “Your shipment is processing. Please wait.”

The product uses “we” only when Almahbub/HAMD has an actual accountable role.
It names external parties-supplier, carrier, customs authority, payment
provider-when their action is the dependency.

## Decision checklist

Before approving a feature or design, the team must answer:

- Does it reduce uncertainty?
- Does it expose trustworthy state and ownership?
- Does it save meaningful effort without removing control?
- Does it work internationally, accessibly, and on constrained devices?
- Is every visual, word, and animation purposeful?
- Is the next action obvious?
- Would removing it reduce a real user outcome? If not, remove it.

## Governance

Product, design, engineering, operations, security, and support jointly own
this DNA. Design reviews must include the checklist. Repeated exceptions,
inconsistent terminology, duplicate workflows, deceptive status language, or
unexplained automation are product-quality defects and must be tracked as such.
